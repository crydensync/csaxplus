# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want taking part in csax+ to be a good experience for anybody, regardless of experience level,
background, identity, or how they got here. We pledge to make participation in this project
harassment-free for everyone.

## What we expect

**Be decent.** Assume the person on the other end is acting in good faith and knows something you
do not. Most disagreements in a project like this are about information, not about character, and
they resolve faster when treated that way.

**Critique the work, not the person.** "This screen shows a number it has not read, here is where"
is useful. "You clearly do not understand this codebase" is not, and it is not made acceptable by
being true.

**Be patient with questions.** Somebody asking what looks like an obvious question is usually
telling you the documentation has a hole in it. That is a gift.

**Respect a decision that has been made.** This project has recorded its decisions with their
reasons in [docs/build/DECISIONS.md](docs/build/DECISIONS.md). If you think one is wrong, argue
with the reasoning and propose a change. Do not quietly work around it.

**Take responsibility.** If you get something wrong, say so, fix it, and move on. Nobody is
expected to be right the first time.

Not acceptable, in an issue, a pull request, a commit message, or anywhere else this project
happens:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory language or imagery, including about race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, gender
  identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, or experience level.
- Sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Publishing somebody's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or sustained disruption after being asked to stop.
- Anything else a reasonable person would call unprofessional in a workplace.

## Scope

This applies in the project's own spaces: issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and
anywhere else you are representing csax+. It applies to how you treat other people there, not to
what you believe in private.

## Reporting and enforcement

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it to the maintainers through GitHub:
open a report on the relevant issue or pull request, or use the repository's **Security** tab if
the report needs to be private. No email address is published for this, deliberately, so that the
report reaches whichever maintainer is active rather than a mailbox that went stale.

Maintainers will look at every report, keep it confidential where they can, and tell the reporter
what they decided. Consequences escalate with severity and repetition:

1. **A private word.** Most things end here, and that is the point of having this file.
2. **A warning**, with a stated boundary and what happens if it is crossed.
3. **A temporary block** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

A maintainer who is the subject of a report does not take part in deciding it. Maintainers who do
not follow this Code of Conduct in good faith face the same consequences as anybody else.
